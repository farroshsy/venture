# EUDR Plot-Level Origin Evidence (Coffee/Cocoa/Palm/Timber)

## Problem & How Truth Is Established Today

EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) due-diligence statements require exporters to submit high-precision plot-level geolocation proving each shipment is deforestation-free (post-Dec 31, 2020 cutoff) and legally produced. In Indonesia, that evidence chain is thin: 98% of coffee area is smallholder plantings averaging 1–2 ha (USDA FAS Coffee Annual, Jakarta, Report ID2025-0021, May 19, 2025); over 60% of palm smallholders lack land certificates (infosawit.com/sawitindonesia.com, 2025); and Koltiva states only ~12% of downstream firms have any traceability system in place (Koltiva/marketersmedia, Jul 24, 2025). Geolocation "evidence" is typically produced one of three ways: (1) exporter-commissioned polygon-mapping contractors (Koltiva and peers) who *collect* coordinates from farmers, often for a fee; (2) government e-STDB (farmer registration), proceeding district-by-district and slowly (Pesawaran district: 1,120 farmers/1,382 ha — kabarsdgs.com, 2026); (3) desk estimation from administrative boundaries where no field survey occurred. None of these routes independently *verifies* a submitted polygon is genuine, unique to one farmer, and not reused across declarations or borrowed from an adjacent permit — collection and verification are conflated today.

## Buyer & Who Pays

EU-facing exporters and traders of coffee, cocoa, palm, and timber who must file a Due Diligence Statement (DDS) per shipment and bear legal/financial exposure (market exclusion, EU customs holds) if a submission is later found fraudulent or non-compliant. This is the buyer for a verification-only wedge; the farmer/smallholder is not the buyer (extension/mapping-cost subsidy is typically paid by the exporter or a program, not the smallholder).

## Documented Failures (cited)

- Only ~1% of 6.2 million ha of Indonesian smallholder palm land is ISPO-certified despite the scheme running over a decade (majalahhortus.com, 2025) — a proxy for low verified-plot documentation; close to, but not confirmed identical to, the brief's specific "1.07% e-STDB, July 2024" figure (**unverified as stated**).
- INDEF survey: 94% of farmers surveyed across three provinces were unaware of EUDR rules (mediaindonesia.com; survey year not confirmed in the retrieved excerpt).
- Koltiva: "only 12% of downstream firms have traceability systems in place," urging "inclusive segregation to prevent smallholder exclusion" (marketersmedia, Jul 24, 2025).
- The regulation's compliance runway moved twice — Dec 2024 → Dec 2025 → Dec 2026 for medium/large operators, Jun 2027 micro/small (Reg. (EU) 2025/2650, per H2 Compliance/PSQR, Dec 2025) — the repeated delay itself signals how far verified-data readiness lags the legal requirement.
- USDA FAS confirms EU (via Belgium/Germany) is a top-2 destination for Indonesian coffee, over 1.4 million bags Mar 2024–Feb 2025 (USDA FAS Coffee Annual ID2025-0021, May 2025). The brief's claim that USDA FAS documented "desk-drawn" coffee polygons could not be confirmed in the pages retrieved (**unverified as stated**).

## Bottom-Up ARR Path (customers x ACV x reachability)

Two possible customer counts: (a) a small number (dozens) of large EU-facing exporters/traders per commodity, who are largely already Koltiva or equivalent customers (Koltiva alone claims 19,000+ upstream/downstream businesses — koltiva.com), making net-new bootstrap capture difficult; or (b) thousands of smaller exporters/cooperatives, where ACV has no clean Indonesian comparable (mapping-service pricing found — Rp150,000–Rp1,000,000/ha, technogis.co.id — is a farmer-side one-time cost, not an exporter-side annual verification-SaaS price). Neither path clears a credible, evidenced >=$1M ARR bar independent of displacing an incumbent that already holds the relationship. **Evidence quality: weak (3).**

## Existing Solutions (named, dated)

- **Koltiva/KoltiTrace** — Indonesia-based; 19,000+ business customers; three modular EUDR products: KoltiTrace (traceability), KoltiSkills (extension), and **Data Verification** ("expertise solutions for comprehensive EUDR analysis reports") (koltiva.com; agreads.com, Feb 2026).
- **National Dashboard Indonesia** — government cross-commodity (palm, coffee, cocoa, rubber) geolocation/traceability aggregator, Kemenko Perekonomian Decision 178/2024, operated by PT Surveyor Indonesia, EU co-funded, launched 2025, explicitly targeting "accurate, reliable, and immutable" traceability/geolocation (rm.id; Mongabay Apr 2025) — criticized by NGOs as insufficiently rigorous (betahita.id).
- **Satelligence** — deforestation-risk mapping, co-produced the first public global coffee map with CIAT (satelligence.com).
- **Orbify / TraceX** — satellite + blockchain EUDR compliance tools, partnered together, active regionally (orbify.com, 2025-2026).
- **Meridia, EFI/KAMI-backed traceability pilots** — smallholder mapping programs (meridia.land; efi.int).

## Kill Tests

- **K1 (Data access): PASS.** For a verification-only wedge, the exporter (paying customer) controls its own DDS submissions and can license third-party satellite/deforestation datasets independently of farmer cooperation.
- **K2 (Oracle bound): PASS.** Deforestation-cutoff satellite imagery, polygon-uniqueness cross-referencing, and boundary-plausibility checks are inferable from existing signals — the same class of evidence EU's own reference system and Global Forest Watch already use.
- **K3 (Absorption): FAIL — KILL.** Koltiva already owns the customer relationship for 19,000+ upstream/downstream businesses and already ships a named "Data Verification" product line (koltiva.com; agreads.com, Feb 2026) — the exact function proposed. The National Dashboard Indonesia (funded, EU co-financed, operator appointed, launched 2025) reinforces this on the government side, explicitly claiming the "accurate, reliable, immutable" verification mandate.
- **K4 (Bootstrap bar): FAIL.** With the addressable exporter base's compliance relationship and budget substantially already held by Koltiva, no independent bottom-up path to $1M ARR survives without displacing that incumbency.

## Scores

- Severity: 7 — documented, multi-source readiness gap (certification rates, awareness survey, traceability-system coverage) with real market-access consequences and a moving compliance deadline.
- Bootstrap revenue quality: 3 — customer base largely already captured by Koltiva; no clean Indonesian ACV comparable for a standalone verification product.
- WTP: 5 — real compliance budget exists but is largely already committed to incumbents.
- Data position: 4 — verification relies on largely public/purchasable satellite datasets, a commodity feed available to any competitor including Koltiva.
- Absorption distance: 3 — adjacent incumbent (Koltiva) already ships the named function; government dashboard reinforces.
- Competition: 3 — funded incumbent (Koltiva) with deep Indonesian/regional traction, plus multiple funded foreign entrants.
- Moat: 4 — mostly per-customer integration lock-in; Koltiva's incumbency blocks new cross-customer network effects.

**Weighted: 4.4**

## Upside Flag

A narrow niche might survive inside Koltiva's own gap: it admits only 12% of downstream firms have traceability systems and warns of smallholder exclusion under strict segregation — a low-cost, farmer-side "verification passport" that plugs into multiple exporters' existing Koltiva/National-Dashboard pipelines (interoperability layer, not a replacement) could be a defensible wedge, but this is secondary-evidence speculation, not independently verified this pass.

## What Would Kill This

Already killed: Koltiva's existing "Data Verification" product line serving 19,000+ businesses, plus the EU-co-funded National Dashboard Indonesia explicitly targeting "accurate, reliable, immutable" geolocation across the same commodities.
