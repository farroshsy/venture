# Geography Test — collateral-existence-monitoring

Bounded test, 2026-07-30. Not a rescoring.

**Stated up front: this candidate is excluded by founder gate G2 (no hardware,
no IoT, no field operations) in every geography, before any market evidence is
weighed.** Verifying that pledged physical inventory actually exists requires
sensing the physical world — sensors, cameras, RFID, or a person walking into a
warehouse. That is not a remote-software product anywhere. The test below was
run anyway, as instructed, to see whether the *market* evidence would have been
different had the gate not applied.

## Comparator markets & why

1. **India.** The largest emerging-market analogue: a mature warehouse-receipt
   and commodity-collateral lending industry, far more lender buyers than
   Indonesia, and an explicit IoT-collateral-monitoring vendor category already
   in existence — the best available test of "does this shape sell elsewhere?"
2. **United States.** The market where post-origination collateral checking is
   a formalised, recurring, separately-billed service (the ABL *field
   examination*), and therefore the market most likely to yield an observable
   price — the exact term Indonesia could not produce.

## Buyer count

- India: **~121 scheduled commercial banks** (2025–26, secondary listing citing
  RBI, 2026); **1,826 of 1,950 DICGC-covered banks are cooperative banks**, and
  NAFCUB supports **~1,500 urban cooperative banks** (RBI *Financial Stability
  Report* 2026 coverage via Indian Cooperative, 2026). The revealed-preference
  number matters more: NCML is "partnered with **56 banks and financial
  institutions** for providing collateral management services across **8,000
  storage structures**" (NCML, ncml.com, 2026). That 56 is the observed count of
  Indian lenders actually buying this category — not thousands.
- US: count of ABL lenders / field-exam buyers not obtained **(unverified)**.

## Observed pricing

**The Indonesian failure travels unchanged — no vendor price found in either
market.**

- India: no published price for NCML or Origo collateral management, and none
  for My Ascents' IoT collateral monitoring (searched 2026-07-30).
- US: no field-examination fee schedule published by any of the firms found
  (Rosenberg & Fecci, LCG Advisors, Freed Maxick, ABLC, CFO Strategies, 2026).
  Borrowers pay exam costs, run semi-annually or quarterly for higher-risk
  credits (Young & Associates; LCG Advisors, 2026) — cadence documented, price
  not. The only public hourly rate located is a regulator's, not a vendor's:
  the **OCC's $137/hour** special-examination fee (OCC, Calendar Year 2026 Fees
  and Assessments notice).
- ABL collateral-monitoring software (Lendscape, Cync, Solifi, timveroOS):
  quote-only, no published pricing (searched 2026-07-30).

## Incumbents & free substitutes

- India: **NCML** (56 lender partners, 8,000 storage structures), plus Origo
  Commodities and StarAgri in the same physical-custody category. **My Ascents
  already sells "Smart Collateral Management — Real Time Inventory Tracking"
  with IoT** (myascents.com, 2026) — the exact IoT/computer-vision product this
  candidate proposed, already occupied. State-adjacent substitute: WDRA-regulated
  negotiable warehouse receipts, structurally the same as Indonesia's SRG.
- US: field-examination firms occupy the human-inspection layer; ABL platforms
  (Lendscape, Cync, Solifi) occupy the borrowing-base software layer; the OCC
  occupies the supervisory layer. No free state substitute in the Indonesian
  sense, but no unoccupied layer either.
- A frequently-quoted "30% of ABL loans default due to inadequate inventory
  oversight" figure traces to a vendor blog citing the National Association of
  Credit Management **(unverified)**.

## Verdict for a solo remote founder

**No — twice over.** (a) **G2 fails by construction** in every geography: the
product's core claim is about physical existence, which no remote seat can
observe. A pure-software variant built on borrower-uploaded photos or video
collapses into a self-attested document, which is precisely the artefact the
product exists to distrust. (b) Even setting G2 aside, the market evidence is
*worse* abroad, not better: India's revealed buyer count for this category is
56 lenders, not thousands, and no vendor price is observable in India or the
US — the identical failure Indonesia produced. **G3** would also bind: warehouse
sensor deployment across even a handful of sites exceeds ~$10k pre-revenue.

## What would change this

Nothing within the founder constraints. This would only become testable if G2
were dropped and a co-founder or partner owned field operations — at which point
it is a different company competing with NCML, My Ascents, Sucofindo and SGS on
physical presence, not a bootstrapped software business.
