# Geography test — Mill FFB Mass-Balance Reconciliation

Bounded test, 2026-07-30. One question only: sold into a comparable-technical
market, would the Bootstrap Bar plausibly be met? No rescoring.

## Comparator markets & why

**Malaysia** is the only serious comparator. It is the sole other market with a
mill population of the same order of magnitude, the same FFB/weighbridge
process, the same EUDR exposure, and a mandatory national sustainability scheme
(MSPO). Anything true of a mill-gate reconciliation product will be true there.

**Thailand, Colombia, Honduras, Guatemala, Papua New Guinea** were considered
and rejected on size before spending search budget: each has a mill population
well below Malaysia's (**unverified** — not researched this pass).

The decisive fact is the global ceiling, not any one country. The **Universal
Mill List** — the industry's shared reference database built by WRI, Rainforest
Alliance, Proforest and Daemeter — mapped **1,815 mills across 26 countries**
(WRI/Global Forest Watch, Mar 2024) and **"more than 2,000 mills" worldwide**
(Global Forest Watch, Jan 2026). Indonesia already holds the largest single
share of that universe. Geography cannot expand this buyer type; it can only
move to a *smaller* slice of the same ~2,000.

## Buyer count

- Malaysia: **450 mills in operation, 119,362,370 t FFB/year capacity (2022)**;
  452 mills in 2019 (MPOB data, published in Kementerian Perladangan dan
  Komoditi *Statistik Komoditi — Sawit 2023*). MPOB's 2024/2025 overview PDFs
  did not load this pass; treat 450 as the working figure.
- Ownership is concentrated among a small number of large listed groups
  (Sime Darby, FGV, IOI, KLK), so the *independent* mid-size mill segment — the
  Indonesian thesis's actual buyer — is a fraction of 450 (**unverified**,
  not quantified this pass).

So the comparator halves the reachable buyer count relative to Indonesia.

## Observed pricing

- **No published price for a mill-gate / weighbridge mass-balance
  reconciliation product was found in any market** (search, Jul 2026). Same
  finding as Indonesia.
- Adjacent EUDR compliance software, published prices: **EUDRReady — free tier,
  €29/month Starter, €79/month Business** (eudrready.eu vendor comparison,
  2026); **Coolset — from approximately €12,000/year** (same source). **osapiens,
  TraceX, DataCross, Sourcemap and Agridence publish no price** and require a
  sales call (eudrready.eu, 2026; vendor sites, Jul 2026).
- Note what those two published prices actually are: a €29-79/month self-serve
  DDS filing tool for small EU importers (wrong buyer, wrong product), and a
  €12k/yr ESG platform. Neither is an observed price a *mill* pays for
  throughput reconciliation.

## Incumbents & free substitutes

Malaysia has the same free state platform Indonesia has. **MSPO Trace**
(MPOCC, launched Nov 2019) is a free public certification and traceability
registry — free mobile app, searchable estate/mill/smallholder supply-chain
database — under a certification scheme that is mandatory in Malaysia
(mspo.org.my; mspotrace.org.my, accessed Jul 2026). That is the National
Dashboard problem, transplanted.

On the EU side the buyer's compliance channel is also state-run and free: DDS
are filed into the Commission's EUDR Information System/TRACES. And the
regulation keeps shrinking the paying population: **Regulation (EU) 2025/2650**
(published Dec 2025) delayed application to **30 Dec 2026** (30 Jun 2027 for
micro/small) and **removed the obligation on downstream operators and traders to
file their own due diligence statements** — they now only retain the primary
operator's reference number (European Commission Access2Markets, 2025; Lexology
analysis of Reg. 2025/2650, Dec 2025). A further simplification package followed
in May 2026. Private vendors: Koltiva, Satelligence, Sourcemap, osapiens, TraceX.

## Verdict for a solo remote founder

**No.** This candidate cannot be rescued by geography because the buyer type is
globally capped at roughly 2,000 organisations and Indonesia already contained
the biggest concentration of them. Malaysia offers ~450 mills, mostly
group-owned, behind procurement, with a free mandatory national traceability
registry already in place and no observable price for the product. The motion is
a physical receiving-dock/weighbridge integration sold to industrial processors —
never self-serve, so **G6 fails** and **G2 is strained** (dock-side data capture
is adjacent to field operations). On top of that the demand driver has slipped
twice and been narrowed by legislative simplification, so even the WTP argument
is less firm abroad than the Indonesian file assumed.

## What would change this

A published price a mill actually pays for gate-level verification (none found
in any market); or evidence that a non-mill buyer with a countable population in
the tens of thousands — EU first-placer importers filing DDS — pays for
*physical-plausibility* reconciliation rather than document collection. That is a
different product with a different buyer.
