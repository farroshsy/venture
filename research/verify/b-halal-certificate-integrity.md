## Verdict: **REFUTED**

Not on the axis the candidate defended. The literal whitespace sentence survives — I found
**no funded Indonesian halal-integrity software vendor**, and searches for halal-tech
startups, halal traceability platforms, halal compliance SaaS, and halal certification apps

## Pillar 1 — K3 fails. Absorption is funded, staffed, and already in motion.

The candidate's K3 PASS rests on one sentence: *"The April 2025 raid was a one-off manual
joint enforcement action, not a funded standing surveillance program; no roadmap,
procurement, or budget for continuous market monitoring was found in this search."*

Every clause of that is contradicted:

- **`Pengawasan JPH Terpadu` is a standing multi-agency programme, not a raid.** `[FULL]`
  BPJPH's own 26 Jul 2026 article describes it as run jointly with the Ministries of Trade,
  Industry and Health; conducted in phases across strategic Jabodetabek points
  ("akan terus kami lakukan secara bertahap di berbagai titik strategis"); inspecting halal
  certification compliance, halal/non-halal product separation, non-halal labelling, meat
  and seafood labelling, modern retail, and KHAS culinary zones. It ran at **1,068 locations**
  in April 2024 `[HEADLINE]` and was still running the week of this scan.
- **`Satgas Halal` is permanent provincial enforcement headcount.** `[HEADLINE]` Kemenag
  task forces documented in Aceh (70 locations), Central Java (35 districts), Maluku,
  Gorontalo, Central and North Sulawesi, West Kalimantan, Riau, South Sumatra — 2024 through
  2026.
- **BPJPH is building permanent regional units right now.** `[HEADLINE]` UPT BPJPH formation
  through 2026: Lampung (Jan), Batam (Feb), West Kalimantan (Mar), Bangka Belitung (May),
  with Bekasi *pengawas halal* already in monitoring-and-evaluation cycles (Jan 2026). This
  is establishment headcount for supervision, not a project.
- **Border screening was institutionalised in 2026.** `[HEADLINE]` BPJPH–Barantin MoU
  (13 May 2026) for integrated biosecurity + halal screening at national entry points,
  triggered by a porcine animal-feed finding (May 2026); import surveillance tightened again
  30 Jul 2026.
- **BPOM already operates the standing surveillance machine the candidate treats as absent.**
  `[HEADLINE]` 56,000 problematic food items found in the Ramadan 2026 intensification alone;
  a new BPOM regulation (6 May 2026) to raise regional surveillance effectiveness. The
  April 2025 porcine finding is explicitly reported as the product of BPOM–BPJPH
  *"sinergi pengawasan"* — i.e. it came **out of** that machine, not instead of it.
- **A state-to-state technology build in the exact function, one week before this scan.**
  `[HEADLINE]` BPJPH–Korea strategic partnership (23 Jul 2026) to develop
  *"teknologi sertifikasi dan logistik halal digital"* — ANTARA, Langit7, pantau.com, same day.
- **The issuance-screening layer is already a state-run automated monopoly.** `[HEADLINE]`
  BPJPH processes **>10,000 halal certificates per day** and won two Top Digital Awards
  (Dec 2025) for the digitalisation that made it possible.

K3's bar is "existing roadmap, announced regulation, active procurement, funded
implementation, or an incumbent already entering the exact function." Four of the five are
independently satisfied. **Absorption distance 5 is not defensible; K3 is a kill, not a pass.**

## Pillar 2 — The announced regulation forecloses the value proposition, and hands the work to LPHs.

The candidate's core asset is *"the certificate is a point-in-time snapshot with no continuous
ingredient-sourcing monitoring."* That is **true today** — and it is true for a specific legal
reason the candidate never states:

> Under UU 6/2023 (Cipta Kerja), an Indonesian halal certificate is valid **seumur hidup** —
> indefinitely — so long as there is no change to ingredient composition, production process,
> ingredient supplier, or production location/facility. `[FULL]` (LPPOM, Dec 2025)

BPJPH's head, Ahmad Haikal Hasan, has publicly diagnosed **exactly the candidate's problem**
and announced **exactly the wrong remedy for the candidate**: `[FULL]` (UKMIndonesia,
10 Oct 2025; corroborated `[HEADLINE]` Tempo, 25 Apr 2025 — "BPJPH Akan Gugat Aturan Sertifikat
Halal Seumur Hidup di UU Ciptaker")

- businesses relocate facilities, add products and change suppliers while the certificate
  stays valid without re-evaluation;
- there is no mechanism to verify standards remain consistent with the original certification;
- foreign buyers and regulators (Saudi Arabia, Malaysia) require renewal every 1–3 years and
  discount Indonesia's indefinite certificates;
- **therefore BPJPH is pushing to revert to a 4-year validity with periodic re-audit.**

Both branches are bad for the candidate:

- **If the revision passes:** the drift gap is closed by a mandated periodic audit performed by
  accredited LPHs with lab access. Recurring revenue flows to the incumbent audit channel. A
  third-party drift-monitoring subscription becomes a lower-authority duplicate of a legal
  requirement. This is textbook K3 "announced regulation."
- **If it does not pass:** certificates remain lifetime — so there is **no renewal cycle, no
  scheduled surveillance audit, and no compliance deadline** that forces any brand to buy
  monitoring. The candidate's moat rationale ("recurring audit/renewal cycles create integration
  lock-in potential") is factually wrong under current law. **Moat 5 → 3.**

The candidate is either absorbed or has no catalyst.

## Pillar 3 — The integrity hole and the money are in different places. WTP 7 does not hold.

**The worst-exposed population has no budget at all.** The self-declare route is where the
integrity failure lives — no lab, no LPH audit. It is also **free to the business and paid from
state budget**: 1.1M free certificates issued in 2025, 1.35M quota for 2026 `[HEADLINE]`, with
the P3H facilitator role itself state-incentivised (Rp81bn disbursed for P3H/LP3H incentives)
`[HEADLINE]`. There is no private money anywhere in the flow that produced Nabidz. Nobody in
that population can buy screening software, and BPJPH — which could — is not a buyer by the
candidate's own concession.

**The LPH segment is smaller and poorer than scored, and its leader builds rather than buys.**

- Count is contested: the candidate says **79** (Kemenko PMK, 2024); Tirto lists **28**
  accredited LPH (11 Apr 2026) `[HEADLINE]`. This discrepancy is unresolved and should be
  resolved before any reliance — but see Pillar 4: the ceiling is small either way.
- The sector spent February 2026 publicly defending its fee structure against **pungli**
  (illegal levy) allegations in halal certification — ALPHI demanding "objective clarification,"
  disclosing cost structures and an Rp1.3bn figure, across Tempo (17 Feb), Sindonews (15 Feb),
  Chanelmuslim (14 Feb), Surya (16 Feb), Mozaik Islam (14 Feb) `[HEADLINE]`. A fee-capped,
  mostly university-affiliated sector under active public price scrutiny is the worst possible
  first-ACV segment.
- **LPPOM, the largest LPH, already runs its own digital stack** — `e-HalalLab`, an online
  laboratory testing platform, plus its own certified-product database `[FULL]`. It is a
  builder, not a customer.

**Brand owners: the cited comparable is for a different function.** WTP 7 requires a named
budget owner *already paying for an inferior alternative* to **this** function. What is evidenced
(GO-HALAL, Ira, KMR at Rp2–5M) is one-time **certification-preparation** spend against a
one-time obligation. A dedicated search for periodic post-certification surveillance audit or
commercial post-cert monitoring in Indonesia (`pengawasan pasca sertifikasi`, `audit surveillance
halal berkala`) returned **essentially nothing** — two off-topic LPPOM explainers. There is no
evidence anyone in Indonesia pays for continuous halal monitoring today.

**The function is already legally assigned to a subsidised human.** Every certified business must
have a **Penyelia Halal** maintaining the SJPH. BPJPH is actively upgrading their competence
(31 Mar 2026: "BPJPH Tingkatkan Kompetensi Penyelia Halal untuk Perkuat Implementasi Sistem
Jaminan Produk Halal") and provincial governments fund the training for free (East Java, four
kabupaten, 2025) `[HEADLINE]`. The buyer's compliant answer to "who watches for drift?" is
already a trained, state-subsidised employee plus a document.

**Sanctions are administrative, not monetary.** PP 42/2024 enforcement is reported as two
administrative sanctions — written warning and withdrawal of goods from circulation `[HEADLINE]`.
No fine surfaced. "Quantified loss avoidance" reduces to recall + reputation, which is what the
certificate itself is already bought to protect.

**WTP 7 → 4 at best.**

## On competition 7 — the claim is literally true and analytically wrong

I could not refute "no funded Indonesian halal-integrity software vendor." Searches for
Indonesian halal-tech startups, halal traceability/blockchain platforms, halal compliance SaaS,
and digital halal-certification facilitation apps returned no commercial player. The only
adjacent artefacts found:

- **Verify Halal (Serunai Commerce, Malaysia)** `[FULL]` — JAKIM-endorsed consumer/retailer
  barcode-and-QR certificate verification; **48 countries, 94 certification bodies, 2,000,000+
  products, ~600K downloads**. No confirmed Indonesia/BPJPH coverage today — but adding BPJPH as
  a 95th body is a data-integration task for them and a company for a new entrant. This occupies
  the consumer/marketplace certificate-authenticity layer regionally.
- **cekhalal.id** `[FULL]` — free lookup aggregating BPJPH certificates, MUI determinations and
  BPOM registrations. No API, no paid tier, no fake-logo detection, no marketplace tooling. It
  demonstrates the lookup layer is already a free commodity.
- **Bare Halal** — global ingredient intelligence; no Indonesian penetration surfaced (unchanged
  from the candidate's own finding).

Under the Program B anchor, competition 7 means "only pilots, abandoned, or foreign-only players
— memo-verified." The verified reason for the emptiness matters more than the emptiness: the
issuance-screening layer is a **state-run automated monopoly inside SIHALAL** (>10,000
certificates/day), and the surveillance layer is a **state enforcement function** staffed by
Satgas Halal, UPT BPJPH, and BPOM. Neither is a procurement market with a named private budget
owner. Per protocol §6, a refuted claim caps the relevant dimension at 4.

## What would revive it (named triggers, for the record)

1. BPJPH's 4-year-validity revision **passes** *and* BPJPH mandates that re-audit evidence be
   supplied through third-party-verifiable supplier/ingredient records rather than an LPH visit.
2. A regulation or platform policy placing **halal-listing liability on marketplaces** — this is
   the single missing hook that would restore segment (c) and with it the ARR path.
3. A monetary penalty regime replacing the current warning/withdrawal sanctions.

None of these has fired as of 30 Jul 2026.

## Surviving gap (narrow, not the claimed venture)

**Export-dossier evidence tooling.** BPJPH's own stated driver for the validity revision is that
Saudi Arabia and Malaysia require renewal every 1–3 years and discount Indonesia's indefinite
certificates `[FULL]`. Indonesian F&B/cosmetics **exporters** therefore face a real, recurring,
**foreign-mandated** re-certification cycle that Indonesian law does not provide domestically —
a genuine deadline, a genuine budget owner, and evidence assembly (supplier changes, ingredient
provenance) that is software-shaped. This is a narrow exporter segment, sold as export-compliance
tooling rather than domestic surveillance, and on the counts available it is unlikely to clear
$1M ARR alone. It should not be scored as the original candidate.
