## 1. K3 Absorption — FAILS

The Program B protocol kills on K3 given "existing roadmap, announced regulation, active procurement, funded implementation, or an incumbent already entering the exact function." Four independent strands are present, and each names the exact function.

### 1.1 The state already ships the per-polygon conformity answer — free, automatic, in minutes

ANTARA, **"RDTR Terintegrasi Percepat PKKPR Otomatis"** (retrieved 30 July 2026, listed as ~18 hours old, i.e. published 29–30 July 2026):

> "Sistem melakukan pencocokan titik koordinat lokasi usaha dengan peta RDTR serta kesesuaian kode Klasifikasi Baku Lapangan Usaha Indonesia (KBLI)"

KKPR is issued "secara otomatis apabila lokasi usaha berada pada wilayah yang telah memiliki RDTR digital yang terintegrasi." Reported performance:

| | Qualifying location | Non-qualifying location |
|---|---|---|
| Turnaround | minutes → max 1 working day | 5–39 working days (manual) |
| Cost | no PNBP fee | PNBP per existing rules |

**570 RDTR integrated with OSS as of 13 May 2026.**

This *is* the product. A polygon goes in; an authoritative conformity determination comes out; it is free; and it is issued by the only actor whose answer has legal effect. The candidate proposed to sell a *risk score* against maps that the state reconciles and adjudicates itself. Against a free, binding, minutes-long state determination, a private probability estimate is not a substitute — it is a strictly weaker artifact.

Crucially this fails K3 on the *relationship* test, not merely the "state could build it" test the protocol explicitly forbids as a kill: every Indonesian business must pass through OSS to hold a permit at all. The absorber already owns the customer, the data, and the legal authority. Coverage expansion (RDTR count) is the only variable, and it is actively funded and increasing.

### 1.2 PP 28/2025 — announced regulation aimed at exactly this overlap

ANTARA, 14 November 2025 (Kemenko Perekonomian): PP 28/2025 (issued 5 June 2025) supersedes PP 5/2021 on risk-based licensing, and is reported as addressing prior "jurisdictional overlap among ministries," introducing SLAs and *fictive-positive* approval. **OSS system adaptation reported 98 percent complete** as of that date.

That is "announced regulation" plus "funded implementation" in the protocol's exact words, in force *now*, aimed at inter-ministry permit overlap.

### 1.3 Stranas PK 2025–2026 Action #1 of 15 — named, funded, and running through the whole 12-month window

ANTARA, 9 December 2024, launch of the National Corruption Prevention Strategy's 15 actions for 2025–2026. Action 1:

> **"Aksi Satu Peta Pengendalian Alih Fungsi Lahan Sawah dan Tumpang Tindih Izin di Kawasan Hutan"**
> (One Map Action: Control of Rice-Field Conversion and **Overlapping Permits in Forest Areas**)

The candidate's function is the literal title of the first action item of a cross-ministry national program with e-monev tracking, whose action period is 2025–2026 — i.e. it spans the entire K3 horizon from the scan date. This is not an adjacent program. It is the same program.

### 1.4 The candidate's own kill condition is already met

The candidate file lists under **"What Would Kill This"**:

> "evidence KSP Geoportal access is opening free to all lenders/corporates"

Confirmed, twice over, and dated *before* the scan:

- **Perpres 28/2023** on data-sharing authority via the Jaringan Informasi Geospasial Nasional expanded access classification beyond senior officials to "individuals, legal entities, businesses," with view/download and view-only tiers (Kompas.id; ANTARA 3 April 2024).
- **Peraturan BIG No. 3/2024** — BIG head Muh. Aris Marfai, quoted in ANTARA 11 July 2024, established "access classification and data-sharing mechanisms for geospatial information" enabling public and business access.
- **Geoportal Kebijakan Satu Peta 2.0** was opened to the public at the One Map Policy Summit, 26–27 June 2024, carrying **158 thematic maps from 24 ministries/agencies and 34 provinces** (ANTARA 3 April 2024; ANTARA 5 August 2024 reports 151 maps from 23 K/L across 38 provinces as of July 2024).

The K3 "PASS" and absorption distance of 4 were scored without this. On the rubric anchors, absorption distance is **3 at best** ("adjacent incumbent/state program exists"), and in truth below the anchor, because 12-month evidence exists rather than being absent.

## 2. Severity is overstated — the baseline is stale and moving the wrong way for the venture

The candidate leads with PITTI's **46.8 million ha / 24.6% of land area** (2021) and asserts it is "still the reference baseline through 2026." The government's own published series contradicts that:

| Date | Overlapping area | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 77 million ha | ANTARA 11 July 2024 (BIG head Aris Marfai) |
| June 2024 | 57 million ha (−10.5% over three years) | same |
| July 2024 | 19.97 million ha of spatial inconsistency reduced | ANTARA 5 August 2024 (Dirjen Virgo Eresta Jaya) |
| End-2024 target | further 9.264 million ha (8.6% of cases) targeted for resolution | ANTARA 3 April 2024 |

IGT compilation is reported 100% complete and integration 98% complete, tracked by e-monev. The problem is being retired by a funded state program with published progress metrics — a shrinking denominator, not a growing one. Severity 8 rests partly on a number the issuing government has since superseded.

## 3. The Scope Test fails on most of the cited loss events

The severity case leans on Satgas PKH: 5.89M ha of palm-sector land, Rp10.27T recovered. But ANTARA's own coverage frames these as recoveries of **unpermitted occupation**, not permit-holder map disputes — "Satgas PKH **kuasai kembali** 4,09 juta hektare lahan hutan dalam setahun" (20 Jan 2026); "Satgas PKH serahkan 2,37 juta hektare lahan kawasan hutan ke negara" (13 May 2026); the enforcement target is described as palm oil **tanpa izin** in forest areas.

Apply the Scope Gate literally: *if uncertainty about the truth disappeared tomorrow, does the customer's reason to buy disappear?* For an operator planting without a permit inside kawasan hutan, there is no uncertainty to resolve — it already knows. The loss is caused by illegality, not by record disagreement. Software that reveals the overlap changes nothing about the seizure. The headline loss figures therefore cannot be transferred to this candidate's severity score.

And the residual genuine disputes are not geometric. Forestry-law expert Sadino (Universitas Al-Azhar Indonesia), ANTARA 10 February 2026, frames the contested cases as turning on **land-tenure origin** and **forest-function classification** (convertible production forest vs. protected forest) — administrative and legal determinations argued before Satgas PKH and the courts. That is an adjudication problem, not an oracle-bounded computation. A private overlap score carries no standing against the state's own map; the operative documents are the state-issued KKPR/PKKPR and the KLHK determination.

## 4. K4 Bootstrap Bar — the ARR path collapses on both terms

**ACV has no local anchor, and the memo says so.** The candidate concedes the $3,000–10,000/year recurring figure is "inferred, not directly evidenced locally." Nothing found in this pass evidences any Indonesian bank or concession holder paying a recurring subscription for permit-overlap risk analytics.

**The comparables cited are not displaceable spend.** KJPP appraisal (Rp10–50M/parcel) and legal DD retainers (Rp50–200M/transaction) are mandated or transaction-triggered professional services. A software risk score does not discharge a bank's appraisal obligation or a law firm's opinion — it would be *additive* cost against a budget line it cannot capture. That is rubric WTP anchor 5 ("budget exists, unowned or slow"), not 7.5.

**The buying behaviour contradicts the recurring model.** The candidate's own evidence is that "overlapping land certificates cannot be used as collateral in banking" — i.e. the bank's response to detected overlap is *binary rejection at origination*. A binary go/no-go decision made once per loan is served by the existing one-time KJPP/notary check and by a free OSS KKPR lookup. It does not generate demand for continuous portfolio monitoring, which is where the entire subscription ARR was supposed to come from.

**Reachable count shrinks too.** The 200–500 corporate figure assumes concession holders will buy a "compliance shield." But the legally meaningful self-regularization pathway for plantations inside forest areas ran under the Job Creation Law regime and its window has closed; post-window, non-compliant operators are in enforcement and litigation, where the state's map governs and private analytics do not help. The willing-to-pay subset is a fraction of the stated count.

## 8. What survives

One narrow, genuinely orthogonal function is left standing, and it is worth logging even though it does not clear the bar:

> **Historical map-version provenance for litigation.** The state's portals (Bhumi, GISTARU, OSS, Geoportal KSP) show *current* state. They do not let a concession holder prove what each ministry's map asserted **on the date its permit was issued**. Reconstructing a time-stamped, court-usable record of prior map versions is something the state has a positive incentive *not* to build, because it would amount to auditing its own record-keeping — this is the rubric's absorption-distance 9 condition ("absorption would require the absorber to act against its own incentives").

But this is litigation-support work sold per dispute to companies already in enforcement, not a bank-facing SaaS subscription. It fails G6/K4 as a bootstrap business: episodic, expert-heavy, adversarial, and dependent on archives nobody has systematically preserved. Log it as a fragment; do not resurrect the candidate on it.
