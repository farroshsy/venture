# Cooperative Member-Fund Integrity Monitoring (KSP + Kopdes)

## Problem & How Truth Is Established Today
A Koperasi Simpan Pinjam (KSP) collects mandatory/voluntary member savings and lends to members. Governance runs through the annual members' meeting (RAT), which rubber-stamps the board's self-prepared financials. External oversight is a reporting relationship, not an audit: cooperatives file data into Kemenkop's Online Data System, and since early 2025 "ODS Mandiri" (Juknis Deputi Kelembagaan No. 1/2025) lets cooperatives update their own records directly — the system, in the words of Program A's prior research, "ingests whatever the cooperative types in." Independent public-accountant audit is mandatory only for KSP/USP with capital ≥Rp5 billion, and even then nothing reconciles the audited figures with real-time bank/cash positions between annual cycles. Under UU No. 4/2023 (P2SK), cooperatives serving non-members ("open loop") must move under OJK supervision, but as of the January 2025 handover only 21 cooperatives — holding a combined Rp339 billion in assets — had transitioned (OJK/Kemenkop joint press release, 2025); the transition is targeted to conclude sometime in 2026 but the vast majority of the sector, now swelled by 80,000 new Koperasi Desa Merah Putih (Kopdes) village cooperatives launched 21 July 2025 with ~Rp5 billion in bank-channelled funding each (KBR, 2025), remains under the same self-declared regime.

## Buyer & Who Pays
Members/depositors bear the losses but have no legal access to the cooperative's bank statements or loan ledgers — the information asymmetry is the fraud's mechanism, not an incidental gap. Cooperative boards/managers control the data but, where fraudulent, have no motive to buy a tool that exposes them. Kemenkop/dinas and (for a sliver of the sector) OJK supervise but run free, self-reported systems. Banks channeling Kopdes credit (Himbara) have loan-covenant leverage over borrowing cooperatives and are the one plausible buyer with both motive and contractual data-access rights — but that collapses to a handful of enterprise accounts, not a self-serve customer base.

## Documented Failures (cited)
- KSP Indosurya: prosecution valued member losses at Rp16 trillion (Viva, 2022 — headline explicitly disputes a higher Rp106 trillion figure sometimes cited elsewhere, unverified provenance); Supreme Court cassation (No. 2113 K/Pid.Sus/2023, 16 May 2023) sentenced founder Henry Surya to 18 years plus a Rp15 billion fine, June Indria to 14 years (Tempo, 2023). Funds were routed to affiliated entities while member statements showed healthy balances.
- Eight problem cooperatives (Indosurya, Sejahtera Bersama, Intidana, Lima Garuda, Timur Pratama, two Pracico entities, Wahana Berkah Sentosa) defaulted on Rp26 trillion of member claims (Kemenkop via Antara/CNBC, 2023); only Rp3.4 trillion repaid under court-homologated plans by 2024 (Tempo, 2024) — Rp22.6 trillion still unpaid.
- Kopdes Merah Putih: a CELIOS survey of 108 village heads across 34 provinces found ~65% saw significant governance gaps, warning of "structured corruption" risk (CELIOS/The Conversation, 2025); a single local report (Sergai, North Sumatra, CNEWS, April 2026 — low reliability, one outlet) alleges a Kopdes construction budget shrank from a stated Rp1.6 billion to ~Rp800 million actually built. Kemenkop has since partnered with Aspenda (regional guarantee-company association) to mitigate Kopdes fraud risk (investortrust.id, 2026).

## Bottom-Up ARR Path
No credible self-serve path found. The population with genuine motive to buy (honest, small cooperatives) has little to prove; the population with the highest severity (Ponzi-style operators) will not buy their own detector. The only buyer with real data-access leverage — Himbara banks channeling Kopdes credit, using loan covenants to compel reporting — is a handful of enterprise accounts, not hundreds of self-serve customers, and pursuing it requires exactly the enterprise-sales motion K4/G6 excludes. Adjacent ACV comparable exists (USSI core-banking maintenance ~Rp500k–2.5M/month per institution, USSI PGS pricing pages 2026) but there is no evidence a cooperative would add a second, assurance-focused line item voluntarily. Evidence quality: weak.

## Existing Solutions (as of mid-2026)
Kemenkop ODS/ODS Mandiri (free, self-reported); OJK licensing for the 21 open-loop cooperatives; PT USSI (IBS/IBSS core banking, 1,730+ institutions including cooperatives), Smartcoop (400+ cooperatives), eKoperasi, Koperasiweb, MyKopkel — all sell bookkeeping/reporting, not independent verification. RUU Perkoperasian (4th amendment) DIM was submitted to DPR Komisi VI on 17 June 2026, proposing an LPS Koperasi (deposit guarantee) and strengthened simpan-pinjam supervision (Antara/Tempo/infobanknews, 2026) — still in Panja deliberation, not enacted. No dedicated startup selling independent, member-facing continuous verification of KSP solvency was found (search repeated across two research phases).

## Kill Tests
- **K1 Data access — FAIL.** The paying customer is either the fraud perpetrator itself (no motive, per Indosurya's affiliated-entity diversion) or an unfunded/narrow state actor (OJK covers 21 of tens of thousands of cooperatives); members/depositors lack any legal or contractual access to bank records.
- **K2 Oracle bound — PASS (conditional).** Fund diversion is inferable from bank/transaction records in principle, but Indosurya's pattern (dressing diversions as related-party loans) means interpretation, not just access, is the harder problem — moot given K1.
- **K3 Absorption — PASS (not killed).** RUU Perkoperasian/LPS Koperasi is only a DIM submitted to DPR in June 2026, not an enacted mandate; no 12-month roadmap evidence found.
- **K4 Bootstrap bar — FAIL.** No reachable self-serve customer segment with both motive and data access; the one buyer with leverage (Himbara) requires enterprise sales.

## Scores
- Severity 9 — Rp26T documented member losses, criminal convictions, sector-existential (Kemenkop 2023; Tempo 2023).
- Revenue 3 — no viable self-serve buyer identified; ACV has no comparable specific to this function.
- WTP 4 — beneficiaries can't pay; state budget exists but slow/narrow (21 institutions).
- Data 3 — the core problem is that evidence is inaccessible without the fraudster's cooperation.
- Absorption 3 — adjacent state program (LPS Koperasi bill) active but unenacted.
- Competition 6 — no dedicated assurance vendor found despite two research-phase searches.
- Moat 5 — cross-cooperative fraud-pattern aggregation plausible in theory, unproven.
- Weighted: 4.9

## Upside Flag
If RUU Perkoperasian enacts LPS Koperasi with mandatory continuous solvency reporting as a condition of deposit-guarantee coverage, a compelled-reporting buyer could emerge — but this is pre-legislative as of July 2026 (secondary evidence only).

## What Would Kill This
Already killed on K1/K4. Would need either (a) LPS Koperasi enacted with a compulsory continuous-reporting mandate creating a forced buyer, or (b) a distribution deal with Himbara making bank-covenant-driven adoption self-serve rather than enterprise-sold.
