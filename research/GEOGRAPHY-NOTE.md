# Geography Note — Appendix to Program B

**Dated 2026-07-30. This is an appendix to `research/PROGRAM-B-CONCLUSION.md`
(frozen), not a new program. It amends nothing in Programs A or B.**

---

## 1. What was tested, and what was not

Programs A and B held geography fixed at Indonesia across 86 candidate slots
and found no survivor, leaving one confound unexamined: was the negative
result about *these opportunities*, or about *Indonesia*?

This note closes that question inside a narrow boundary. The nine candidates
that passed all four kill tests and died on the 7.0 bar (PROGRAM-B-CONCLUSION
§5, §9.2) were re-examined in the strongest comparator market identifiable,
asking one question:

> Would the Bootstrap Bar plausibly be met if the identical product were sold
> elsewhere?

**Explicitly not done.** No rescoring — no rubric applied, no score
recomputed, no matrix cell touched. No new candidates sourced. No protocol was
pre-registered, and none is claimed. The nine files in `research/geography/`
are desk memos, not candidate profiles, and carry no evidentiary standing
under the Program B protocol. This is Program B's own §9.2 question answered
once, cheaply, so it is not rediscovered as new work.

Founder gates G1–G6 were applied as written, because the question is
counterfactual about a *specific solo remote founder*, not about the market in
the abstract.

## 3. Verdict on the premise

**The Indonesian negative result is not caused by Indonesian market structure.
It reproduces elsewhere — and the mechanism of reproduction is not the one
Program B named.**

Two things must be separated cleanly.

**What *is* Indonesia-specific.** Two of Program B's stated failure terms are
genuine artefacts of Indonesian market thinness and do reverse abroad. Buyer
count reverses in six of nine cases, sometimes by two orders of magnitude
(1.53 crore GSTINs vs 150–250 Indonesian PKP finance teams). Price
observability reverses in six of nine — Indonesia's "no observable price"
finding (§6 of the conclusion) is substantially a small-market artefact.

**What is not.** In every case where those two terms reversed, the candidate
still failed, because the binding constraint was never buyer count or price
*visibility*. It was price *level*, incumbent density, and above all the sales
**motion** — gate G6. Seven of the nine die at least partly on G6; the pattern
is remorselessly consistent. In developed markets, higher willingness to pay
is *purchased with* procurement: a BAA, SOC 2, vendor security review, model
risk documentation. Every ACV large enough to reach $1M ARR with a reachable
customer count sits behind that wall, and every tier that is genuinely
self-serve has been commoditised to $0.33–$14 per transaction by funded
vendors. That is the structural finding, and it is general, not Indonesian.

One correction to the frozen record's *interpretation* (not its verdict):
Program B read the free state substitute as an Indonesian pathology. **It is
not.** UK Companies House serves the register free by API; GSTN's IMS gives
Indian buyers per-invoice reject rights free; RBI distributes MuleHunter.AI
free to 23 banks; SSA's eCBSV is statutory and cheap; NPDB is $2.50; MSPO
Trace is free and mandatory. Richer states ship *more* free substitution, not
less. Program B's §6 conclusion holds; its implied geographic explanation does
not.

So the honest formulation: **the constraint is partly Indonesia-specific, and
the founder constraint bites just as hard elsewhere — harder.** A bigger
market bought bigger incumbents and a heavier sale, not an easier one.

## 4. What travels

**One candidate, marginally: `beneficial-ownership-verification`.**

The only case where arithmetic and motion both work. Certivus sells this
function to UK accountants self-serve at £49/149/499 per month with no sales
contact — so $1M ARR is roughly 435 firms at the middle tier against 50,000+
EU notaries plus tens of thousands of accountancy practices. A G6-compatible
shape, demonstrated by a live competitor rather than inferred.

What would have to be true for it to become a "yes":

1. A single named EU jurisdiction — Italy, Spain or Poland — with a large
   notariat and a **pre-2027** national BO verification duty already in force,
   solving the G1 twelve-month window that AMLR's 10 Jul 2027 start date
   otherwise breaks.
2. At least one competitor publishing self-serve pricing *in that language*,
   confirming the motion survives localisation.
3. A defensible answer to regulatory-erasure risk, documented twice in twelve
   months (FinCEN erased ~32M US buyers Mar 2025; NY vetoed its own domestic
   scope Dec 2025) — precisely the shock a solo founder cannot absorb.
4. A position against the crowded live UK market, where Certivus, SmartSearch,
   Thirdfort, First AML, Amiqus and Xama already sell.

None of that was searched jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction. "Marginal" means *not
falsified*, not *validated*.

## 5. Limits

Desk research, one pass, no customer interviews — the same standard as
Programs A and B, with less rigour, since no protocol governed it. Several
buyer counts are unverified (Indian private hospitals, GCC institutions, UK
accountancy totals, US staffing/screening firms). Comparator selection was
judgment, not enumeration: the Philippines went untested for
`cross-registry-credential-platform`, named EU jurisdictions for the one
marginal candidate. Negative competitor findings are weak for the same reason
they were weak in Program B.

The largest unmeasured term is not in the table. **A solo remote Indonesian
founder selling into US, UK or EU regulated buyers faces trust, procurement,
data-residency, contracting and payment friction that this note cannot
quantify and did not attempt to.** Every "marginal" above is a ceiling that
assumes that friction is zero. It is not zero, and it runs in one direction
only — against the candidate.

## 6. What this does not license

- It does **not** reopen Program A or Program B. Both remain frozen. No score,
  kill-test verdict, or conclusion in either is amended by this note.
- It does **not** promote `beneficial-ownership-verification` to a build
  decision, a shortlist, or a survivor. It failed the Program B bar in
  Indonesia and that result stands.
- A travelling candidate would require **its own new, versioned program with a
  pre-registered protocol, hypothesis and rubric** — per PROGRAM-B-CONCLUSION
  §10 — before any code is written. Nine desk memos are a reason to consider
  opening such a program, not a substitute for one.
- Nothing here changes §9.1's standing recommendation: a **budget-first
  search** remains the more promising unexplored direction, and this note
  strengthens that case rather than weakening it.
